Anchor

Privacy Policy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Anchor is open-source classroom software made by teachers. It is built to enforce focus during a lesson, not to surveil students — it only observes activity while a teacher has an active focus session running, and the data it produces stays inside the school that operates it.

What Anchor is

Anchor is a school-internal focus-session system for managed and bring-your-own-device classroom laptops. A teacher starts a session for a class; for its duration each student's device softly enforces a teacher-defined allowlist of apps and websites, and off-list activity is shown live on the teacher's dashboard. This policy covers the Anchor Microsoft Edge extension and the on-device agent it works with.

Who controls your data

Anchor is backend-agnostic and self-hosted: each school or organisation runs its own Anchor backend, and the extension is told which backend to use by the agent installed on the device. The school that deploys Anchor is the data controller and decides how the data is stored, retained, and used, under its own policies and applicable law.

Plink Labs publishes the software as open source. Plink Labs does not operate a backend, does not receive any data the extension collects, and has no access to any school's data.

What the extension collects, and when

The extension only inspects or transmits anything while a teacher-started focus session is active. Outside an active session it inspects no URLs and sends no data. During an active session, it sends the following to the school's Anchor backend:

  • Off-allowlist page visits — when a student navigates to a page that is not on the session's allowlist, the page's URL and hostname, the time it occurred, and an internal browser tab identifier. On-allowlist browsing is not recorded.
  • Unblock requests — if a student asks the teacher to allow a blocked site, the hostname being requested.
  • Integrity signals — events that indicate the enforcement may have been bypassed during the session (for example, opening a private/InPrivate window, revoking the extension's site access, or the on-device agent becoming unavailable).
  • Session liveness — periodic heartbeats indicating the extension is still running for the active session.

This activity is associated with the signed-in student for the duration of the session so the teacher can see which student's device is off task. The extension does not record keystrokes, page contents, screenshots, passwords, or any browsing that happens outside an active session.

What the agent collects, and when

The agent only watches which app is in the foreground while a teacher-started focus session is active. During an active session, it sends the following to the school's Anchor backend:

  • App switches — each time a different app comes to the foreground: the program's name (for example msedge or WINWORD), its publisher, and whether it was blocked. It does not send the window title, the file path, or anything shown in the window.

Diagnostic logs (optional)

A school can switch on diagnostic log uploads, which are off by default. The agent then uploads its own log lines from 30 minutes before each session starts to 10 minutes after it ends, for the students on that session's roster and for nothing else. The log lines are technical: sign-in and connection status, updates, errors, which apps the agent let through, paused or blocked during the session (by program name), and the hostnames of off-list sites it handled. They include the student's school account name. They contain no window titles, page contents, keystrokes, screenshots, or passwords. Before and after the session itself they hold only technical messages, because the agent doesn't watch apps then.

Only the administrators of the school's Anchor backend can download diagnostic logs; teachers can't.

Where data is stored locally

On the device, the extension keeps only what it needs to run: the current session's allowlist and metadata (cleared when the session ends or the browser restarts) and the backend address supplied by the agent. It does not maintain a local browsing history.

The agent keeps its own log files in the student's Windows profile for 14 days, whether or not the school has switched on diagnostic log uploads. They hold the same kind of technical lines described above.

How the data is used

Session data (the extension's reports and the agent's app switches) is used for Anchor's core purpose: enforcing the focus session and giving the supervising teacher live visibility of off-task activity during that session.

Diagnostic logs may be used for only two purposes:

  • Technical diagnosis — finding out why Anchor did or didn't block something, or why a device dropped out of a session.
  • A concrete suspicion of fraud during a test or exam — only at the request of the school's leadership, and limited to the students and the session that the suspicion concerns.

They are not used for routine checks or to monitor students in general.

None of this data is used for advertising, sold or rented, shared with third parties, used to build profiles beyond the classroom context, or used to assess creditworthiness.

Data retention

Because each school operates its own backend, retention and deletion are governed by that school's configuration and policies. By default the backend deletes raw session events after 14 days and diagnostic log uploads 14 days after they arrive; it keeps per-session summaries longer. Questions about how long your data is kept, or requests to access or delete it, should be directed to your school or its IT administrator, who controls the Anchor deployment.

Children's privacy

Anchor is used in schools, including with students who may be minors, under the authority and direction of the school. The school is responsible for the lawful basis (including any required parental notice or consent) for using Anchor with its students. A school that switches on diagnostic log uploads should tell students and parents that it does, and for which purposes. Plink Labs does not knowingly collect data directly from children — it operates no backend and receives no data.

Open source

Anchor's full source code, including exactly what the extension and the agent collect and send, is public and auditable at github.com/plinklabs/Anchor.

Changes to this policy

If the data Anchor collects changes, this policy will be updated and the "last updated" date above revised. Material changes will be reflected in the published extension listing.

Contact

For questions about Anchor itself, open an issue at github.com/plinklabs/Anchor. For questions about how your specific school handles Anchor data, contact your school or its IT administrator.
