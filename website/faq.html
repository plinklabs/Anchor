Anchor

Frequently asked questions

The questions teachers and IT leads ask first. Anchor is open-source classroom software made by teachers — it keeps devices to the lesson, it doesn't keep watch on students. Where the privacy policy has the details, we link to it; where a school's own setup decides the answer, we say so.

Privacy & surveillance

Is this spyware? Does it watch students all the time?

No. Anchor only does anything while a teacher has a focus session running — outside a session it inspects nothing and reports nothing. During a session, the agent and the extension decide on the device which apps and pages may open, and those decisions aren't reported to the teacher (the pilot builds still report some, see below). The teacher is told only what the next answer lists. Anchor does not record keystrokes, page contents, screenshots or passwords.

What does the teacher see, and what is sent for it?

During a session the teacher sees:

  • who joined, declined or left the session, or quit the agent;
  • access requests: the page a student asks to open (its address and site), sent only when the student clicks Request access;
  • tamper signals (such as an InPrivate window left open, the Anchor extension switched off, or its access to websites taken away) and laptops that lose their connection or never join.

The teacher does not see which apps a student used or which blocked pages a student tried to open. The home page explains why.

For this, the student's device sends the school's Anchor backend those events, tied to the signed-in student, and a regular heartbeat so that a laptop that drops out is noticed. The privacy policy lists exactly what is sent, every kind of tamper signal included, and the source is public so you can verify it.

Is that what the pilot builds do?

Not yet. To test the enforcement in real classrooms, the builds used in the current pilot also report each app that comes to the foreground, the site of each page the extension blocks, and sites that aren't allowed but were open in Edge without being blocked. The teacher sees these on the session's page in History, and the last one also in the live feed. These reports will be removed before Anchor is used in real lessons. The privacy policy lists exactly what each one contains.

Can the school look back at what a student did?

Only in a narrow way. Besides the session data above, a school can switch on diagnostic log uploads, which are off by default. Only the administrators of the school's backend can download them, and the privacy policy allows two uses: technical diagnosis, and a concrete suspicion of fraud during a test or exam, only at the request of the school's leadership. A school that switches them on should tell students and parents.

Do students need to consent, and is that handled?

Anchor runs in schools under the school's authority, so the school is the data controller and is responsible for the lawful basis — including any parental notice or consent its policies or local law require. Practically, students always know a session is running: they sign in with their existing school account, and the agent shows a join confirmation when a session starts.

How it works

Can a student bypass it?

Yes — deliberately. Anchor is soft enforcement, not a lockdown, on managed and bring-your-own laptops alike: a motivated student without admin rights can still step around it. What the teacher gets then is a signal, such as that the student quit the agent or switched the extension off, not a log of what the student did. The home page explains why it's built that way.

What happens when a student goes off task?

Off-list apps and pages are set aside, without the student losing their work, and the block page lets them ask for access; the teachers' page shows what students see. The teacher isn't told about either. They hear about a site only when the student asks for access to it. (The pilot builds report more: see above.)

Does it keep running between lessons?

No. A session ends when the teacher ends it, or when it reaches the end the teacher chose when starting it, such as the end of this or the next lesson period; a session still running after four hours ends by itself as a safety net. Enforcement and reporting end with it.

Platforms

What platforms does it support? Chromebooks, Macs, phones?

Students need a Windows laptop (Windows 10 version 1809 or later, or Windows 11) with Microsoft Edge, school-issued or their own. There is no Mac, Chromebook, iPad or phone version for students, by design. The teacher dashboard is a website that runs in any modern browser. The IT page has the full requirements.

Do students have to install anything?

On a school-managed laptop, no: the school's IT installs the agent and the Anchor extension. On their own laptop, a student runs the agent installer their school publishes, once; it needs no admin rights, and the agent then helps them add the extension. After that a student installs nothing and manages no extra password — they sign in with their existing school Microsoft account. The IT page describes the rollout.

Cost & data

Does it cost anything?

Anchor itself is free and open source, licensed GPL-3.0. There is no licence fee and no per-seat charge; Plink Labs sells nothing. The only cost is hosting your own backend in your school's Azure subscription: about €24 a month for a school of around 1,000 students. The IT page lists the Azure plans behind that figure, and when it costs more.

Who can see the data, and where is it stored?

Anchor is self-hosted: each school runs its own backend in its own Azure subscription, and student devices and the dashboard talk only to that. Your data stays in your Azure. The teacher who runs a session sees what's listed above for their class, live and afterwards in History. If the school switches on diagnostic log uploads, only its backend administrators can download them. Access beyond that is governed by the school, which is the data controller. Plink Labs operates no backend, receives no student data, and has no access to any school's deployment.

How long is the data kept?

Retention is set by the school, because the school runs the backend. As a default the backend prunes raw session events after 14 days and keeps summaries, and it deletes diagnostic log uploads 14 days after they arrive, but each deployment can configure this. The privacy policy lists each kind of data and how long it is kept. Requests to access or delete data should go to your school or its IT administrator, who controls the deployment.

Can we audit what it actually collects?

Yes. Anchor's full source code — including exactly what each component collects and sends — is public at github.com/plinklabs/Anchor. Nothing about the data flow is hidden.
